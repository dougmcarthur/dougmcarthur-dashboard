Sun Dogs Music Scout is a research and drafting tool, and it never acts for you

It is built for working Canadian musicians. It lists and ranks opportunities such as festivals, showcases, grants and sync pitches, reminds you of deadlines, estimates what a trip would cost, keeps your answers and documents in one place, and drafts text for you to use. Costs are in Canadian dollars and the notes on paperwork assume a Canadian act. You may still look at an opportunity anywhere in the world.

It never submits an application, sends a pitch or any other message to an organiser, accepts an offer or pays a fee for you. A draft is text for you to read, change and send yourself. The Gmail permission creates drafts in your Drafts folder. The service has no way to send a message, even though Google’s permission would allow it.

Ranking and flags help you decide what to look at first. Every decision is yours, and nothing about you is decided by a machine.

You are the sender of everything you send and the publisher of everything you publish. You are responsible for what it says and for following the law and the organiser’s rules, including Canada’s anti-spam legislation where it applies to a message to someone who has not asked to hear from you.

What the service tells you may be wrong, so check it before you rely on it

Treat what the service shows as a starting point for your own checking, not as the last word.

  • Listings and deadlines: opportunities are found by software agents and read from public web pages. A deadline, fee, eligibility rule or address can be wrong, out of date or missing, and a closing date recovered from prose is shown as approximate. Confirm it on the organiser’s own page before you apply or pay anyone.
  • Whether a target takes pitches: the service reads an organisation’s site and tells you when it asks for no unsolicited submissions. It can miss a refusal, and finding none is never permission. You can pitch anyway, and that is your decision.
  • Costs and distances: a trip cost is a range built partly from guesses, not a quote. Distances are measured with OpenStreetMap data and can be off.
  • Paperwork and timing: notes about visas, work permits, tax forms and contracts are general information. They are not legal, immigration, tax or financial advice, so ask a qualified person before you rely on them.
  • AI: software agents running on AI models write parts of what you see, such as research notes and draft text. They make mistakes. Read a draft before you use it.
  • Organisers: a listing is not an endorsement. We do not vet organisers, and we cannot promise that any of them is honest, fair or will pay you. Be careful with anyone who asks you for money before they will consider you.

If you run a festival, label or program and a listing about you is wrong, write to us and we will correct or remove it.
